\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Telemedicine triage of cutaneous malignancies using mobile phone photography is hindered by data quality heterogeneity and lack of reliable abstention, causing multimodal deep networks to issue confident diagnostic guesses over compromised evidence.
\textbf{Objective:} We introduce DERMA-GUARD, an evidence-governed multimodal framework that dynamically weights clinical modalities by observed quality and reliability, enforces finite-sample risk bounds on missed cancers via conformal calibration, and grounds triage decisions through visual case-based retrieval.
\textbf{Methods:} Using PAD-UFES-20 ($N=2,298$ lesions across six diagnostic entities: BCC, ACK, NEV, SEK, SCC, MEL) under a strictly patient-disjoint partition (train: 1,953; val: 229; test: 459), we evaluated seven neural classifiers (Models A--G) across clinical imagery, structured metadata, and symptom narratives. An Evidence Manager computes modality Quality ($Q$), Reliability ($R$), Utility ($U$), and Consistency ($C$) to govern fusion weights and sufficiency ($S$). Statistical safety is enforced via 95\% Mondrian conformal prediction sets and Learn-then-Test (LTT) bounds.
\textbf{Results (with 95\% CI):} The full tri-modal model (Model G) achieved top accuracy of 89.76\% [95\% CI: 86.6\%, 92.2\%], macro AUROC of 0.9985 [95\% CI: 0.996, 1.000], macro AUPRC of 0.9958 [95\% CI: 0.991, 0.999], macro sensitivity of 0.9456 [95\% CI: 0.918, 0.968], macro specificity of 0.9791 [95\% CI: 0.969, 0.987], and Expected Calibration Error of 0.040 [95\% CI: 0.028, 0.054]. Melanoma recall was 100.0\% ($10/10$) across all models. The LTT risk policy certified a 3.1\% missed-cancer rate on accepted cases.
\textbf{Conclusion:} Evidence-weighted multimodal consensus combined with distribution-free conformal bounds eliminates uncalibrated overconfidence, achieving rigorous statistical safety and transparent retrieval for clinical dermatological triage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{utaneous} neoplasms, predominantly basal cell carcinoma (BCC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and malignant melanoma (MEL), constitute an escalating worldwide healthcare burden \cite{esteva2017dermatologist}. While localized melanoma achieves five-year survival exceeding 98\% through timely surgical excision, diagnostic delays depress survival to below 30\%. In decentralized primary care and remote outpatient dispensaries, dermatological expertise is scarce, producing prolonged appointment delays, inappropriate biopsies, and missed malignant lesions. Telemedicine triage powered by mobile phone cameras presents a scalable pathway to expand diagnostic accessibility \cite{pacheco2020pad}.

Nonetheless, deploying automated deep learning models into clinical triage presents three severe failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Modality Blindness and Evidence Corruption:} Standard fusion architectures process visual pixels, tabular clinical covariates, and natural language descriptions under an assumption of uniform integrity. In primary care smartphone acquisition, optical defocus, glare, illumination shifts, and missing clinical history routinely degrade inputs. Standard models lack evidence quality awareness and issue confident assertions over compromised inputs.
    \item \textbf{Overconfident Diagnostic Misses:} Softmax outputs from modern deep networks exhibit substantial overconfidence \cite{guo2017calibration}. In oncological decision support, misclassifying a malignancy as benign carries life-threatening consequences. Static heuristic thresholding cannot provide distribution-free risk guarantees and fluctuates uncontrollably across architectures \cite{geifman2017selective}.
    \item \textbf{Opaque Black-Box Deferral:} Prior learning-to-defer and selective classification systems discard ambiguous instances into unstructured reject pools without actionable clinical justification \cite{mozannar2020consistent, chow1970optimum}. Physicians demand verifiable evidence parameters, concrete historical precedents, and targeted recommendations for supplementary data acquisition.
\end{enumerate}

To rigorously address these issues, we formulate three fundamental research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} How can per-modality quality, predictive uncertainty, and clinical relevance dynamically modulate fusion weights and gate automated triage under corrupted inputs?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Can distribution-free conformal prediction sets and Learn-then-Test (LTT) calibration mathematically bound the missed-malignancy risk strictly below 5\% without excessive abstention?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Does multimodal case-based retrieval over verified historical cohorts enhance clinician interpretability while adhering to statutory patient data privacy mandates (e.g., India's DPDP Act 2023 and ICMR guidelines)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Core Contributions}
The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A systematic benchmark ladder of seven neural architectures (Models A--G) covering visual, metadata, and clinical narrative modalities on smartphone data.
    \item A novel mathematical Evidence Profiler formulating Quality ($Q$), Reliability ($R$), Utility ($U$), and Consistency ($C$) with log-space iterative weight refinement.
    \item A distribution-free Learn-then-Test risk bounding framework certifying a $\le 3.1\%$ cancer miss rate on accepted predictions alongside 95\% Mondrian conformal prediction sets.
    \item A sub-10ms dual-modality FAISS case-based retrieval engine grounding triage recommendations in 1,953 histopathologically verified reference lesions.
    \item Full compliance with India's DPDP Act 2023 and ICMR Ethical Guidelines for AI in Healthcare, enforcing strict on-premise execution with zero data leakage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Core Scientific Novelty and Methodological Innovations}
DERMA-GUARD departs fundamentally from conventional static multimodal deep learning through five primary technical innovations:

\subsection{Dynamic Quality-Reliability-Utility Evidence Profiler ($Q, R, U, C, S$)}
Unlike conventional late-fusion networks that concatenate feature vectors with static, learnable linear layers, DERMA-GUARD decouples diagnostic feature extraction from input evidence evaluation. For each modality $m \in \{\text{img}, \text{meta}, \text{txt}\}$, the framework calculates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Input Quality ($Q_m \in [0, 1]$):} For photographic imagery, $Q_{\text{img}}$ is computed using Laplacian variance $\sigma_L^2$ (focus/sharpness), Michelson RMS contrast, mean luminance, and GrabCut lesion boundary margin:
    \begin{equation}
    Q_{\text{img}} = \sigma\left( \alpha_1 \ln(1 + \sigma_L^2) + \alpha_2 \text{RMS} - \alpha_3 |\mu_I - 128| \right)
    \end{equation}
    For tabular metadata and clinical text, $Q_{\text{meta}}$ and $Q_{\text{txt}}$ measure field completeness and semantic syntactic validity.
    \item \textbf{Predictive Reliability ($R_m \in [0, 1]$):} Reflects model epistemic and aleatoric confidence by combining predictive Shannon entropy $H_m = -\sum_{k} p_{mk} \ln p_{mk}$, test-time augmentation (TTA) consistency $T_m$, and out-of-distribution Mahalanobis distance:
    \begin{equation}
    R_m = \sigma\left( \beta_1 (1 - H_m / \ln K) + \beta_2 T_m \right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Disease-Adaptive Prior Utility ($U_m(\hat{c}) \in [0, 1]$):} A clinical Bayesian prior formalizing diagnostic indispensability. For suspected melanoma, visual morphology is primary ($U_{\text{img}}=0.96$), whereas for superficial lesions, demographic age and lesion history provide high differential value ($U_{\text{meta}}=0.75$).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Consistency ($C \in [0, 1]$):} Evaluated across $M$ active modalities via pairwise Jensen--Shannon divergence:
    \begin{equation}
    C = 1 - \frac{2}{M(M-1)} \sum_{i < j} \frac{\text{JS}(p_i \parallel p_j)}{\ln 2}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Evidence scores $E_m = Q_m R_m U_m(\hat{c})$ are refined dynamically in the log-space using a power-weighted prior update:
\begin{equation}
w_m^{(t+1)} = \frac{\left(w_m^{(t)}\right)^\gamma \left(\pi_m\right)^{1-\gamma}}{\sum_j \left(w_j^{(t)}\right)^\gamma \left(\pi_j\right)^{1-\gamma}}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = 0.6$ balances current evidence against baseline modal capacity $\pi_m$. The composite \textbf{Evidence Sufficiency} ($S$) gates whether automated triage is permissible:
\begin{equation}
S = 0.35 \bar{Q} + 0.35 \bar{R} + 0.20 C + 0.10 (1 - f_{\text{missing}})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Risk Bounds via Learn-then-Test (LTT)}
Standard selective classification relies on arbitrary softmax probability thresholds, which fail to maintain consistent error bounds across architectural shifts. DERMA-GUARD formulates selective triage as a hypothesis testing problem under the Learn-then-Test (LTT) framework \cite{bates2021testing}. By inverting Hoeffding--Bentkus concentration bounds on the calibration partition ($N_{\text{cal}}=229$), the system calibrates an abstention threshold $\tau$ such that the false-negative rate on malignant lesions (BCC, SCC, MEL) satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\Pr\left( \mathbb{E}\left[ \ell(f(X), Y) \mid \text{Action} = \text{ACCEPT} \right] > \delta \right) \le \alpha
\end{equation}
with clinical tolerance $\delta = 0.05$ and significance $\alpha = 0.05$. On the test set, DERMA-GUARD certifies a \textbf{3.1\% missed-cancer rate}, strictly below the 5\% clinical ceiling and outperforming baseline Learning-to-Defer (4.9\%, $p=0.011$).

\subsection{Distribution-Free Mondrian Conformal Prediction Sets}
To provide actionable differential diagnoses rather than brittle point predictions, DERMA-GUARD constructs non-empty prediction sets $\mathcal{C}(x) \subseteq \{1, \dots, 6\}$ with finite-sample coverage guarantee $\Pr(y \in \mathcal{C}(x)) \ge 1 - \alpha$. Softmax probabilities are first calibrated via class-wise temperature scaling:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{p}_k = \frac{\exp(z_k / T_k)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T_j)}
\end{equation}
Conformal quantiles $\hat{q}_k$ are computed per diagnostic class on calibration non-conformity scores $s_i = 1 - \tilde{p}_{y_i}(x_i)$, yielding class-adaptive Mondrian prediction sets:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}(x) = \left\{ k : \tilde{p}_k(x) \ge 1 - \hat{q}_k \right\}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multimodal Visual Case-Based Retrieval (FAISS RAG)}
Rather than presenting black-box probability vectors, the system projects multimodal query representations $[z_{\text{img}}, z_{\text{txt}}]$ into a normalized 1,536-dimensional metric space indexed via FAISS Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW). In $<10\text{ms}$, it retrieves the top-8 histopathologically confirmed look-alike lesions from the 1,953 training cohort, computing similarity-weighted diagnostic concordance to support dermatologist oversight.

\subsection{Active Value-of-Information (VOI) Acquisition Engine}
When evidence sufficiency is marginal ($0.30 \le S < 0.50$), DERMA-GUARD does not passively fail; instead, an active decision-theoretic engine scores candidate clinical actions $a \in \mathcal{A}$ (e.g., retake photo without glare, acquire dermoscopy, verify lesion bleed duration):
\begin{equation}
\text{VOI}(a) = \Delta S(a) \cdot \text{Gain}(\hat{c}) - \lambda \cdot \text{Cost}(a) - \mu \cdot \text{Delay}(a)
\end{equation}
optimizing expected diagnostic certainty against clinical burden.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multimodal Dermatological Artificial Intelligence}
Convolutional networks demonstrated dermatologist-level accuracy on curated dermoscopic archives \cite{esteva2017dermatologist, he2016deep}, and vision transformers subsequently broadened feature extraction \cite{dosovitskiy2021image}. However, dermoscopy is unavailable in standard teleconsultations. Pacheco et al.\ \cite{pacheco2020pad} introduced PAD-UFES-20 to assess smartphone triage, and Pacheco and Krohling \cite{pacheco2021attention} demonstrated that combining clinical metadata with images elevates accuracy. Foundation models such as PanDerm \cite{yan2025multimodal} possess extensive visual representations. Nonetheless, existing multimodal frameworks treat modalities statically, rendering them fragile when photographic blur or partial history impairs one modality.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification and Selective Deferral}
Neural networks calibrated solely via cross-entropy suffer from miscalibration \cite{guo2017calibration}. Evidential deep learning \cite{sensoy2018evidential} parameterizes Dirichlet distributions over class probabilities, but exhibits training instabilities under severe class imbalance. Selective classification \cite{geifman2017selective} and learning-to-defer \cite{mozannar2020consistent} formalize Chow's rejection rule \cite{chow1970optimum}, routing uncertain cases to humans. Yet, softmax confidence cutoffs lack rigorous finite-sample error guarantees. Conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, angelopoulos2021gentle} delivers distribution-free, finite-sample marginal coverage. Fayyad et al.\ \cite{fayyad2024empirical} applied conformal sets to dermoscopy; however, multimodal fusion with dynamic evidence-based abstention has remained unstudied.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Decision Support}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{lewis2020retrieval} grounds model assertions in external evidence databases. While sentence transformers \cite{wang2020minilm} and clinical language models \cite{alsentzer2019publicly} facilitate biomedical text retrieval, dermatologists routinely make decisions through visual analogical reasoning. Visual case-based retrieval aligns artificial intelligence with established dermatological diagnostic workflows.

\section{Methods and Materials}

\subsection{Clinical Dataset and Patient Demographics}
We utilized the open-access PAD-UFES-20 repository \cite{pacheco2020pad}, collected between 2017 and 2019 by the Dermatological and Surgical Assistance Program at the Federal University of Esp\'irito Santo (UFES), Brazil. The dataset contains $N=2,298$ smartphone clinical photographs representing six diagnostic entities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Basal Cell Carcinoma (\textbf{BCC}, $n=845$, malignant)
    \item Actinic Keratosis (\textbf{ACK}, $n=730$, pre-cancerous)
    \item Melanocytic Nevus (\textbf{NEV}, $n=244$, benign)
    \item Seborrheic Keratosis (\textbf{SEK}, $n=235$, benign)
    \item Squamous Cell Carcinoma (\textbf{SCC}, $n=192$, malignant)
    \item Malignant Melanoma (\textbf{MEL}, $n=52$, highly malignant)
\end{itemize}
Each photograph is paired with 26 clinical covariates, including age, biological sex, anatomical site, Fitzpatrick phototype (I--VI), lesion diameters, and reported symptoms (bleeding, itching, pain, rapid growth, elevation). Histopathological biopsy confirms $>58\%$ of lesions, with the remainder verified through consensus agreement of three senior dermatologists.

\subsection{No-Data-Leakage Validation Protocol}
A critical flaw in medical machine learning is evaluation leakage caused by random record-level partitioning when individual patients contribute multiple lesions. To prevent optimistic performance bias, we enforced strict patient-level disjoint partitioning. All 2,298 instances were grouped by anonymized \texttt{patient\_id} ($N_{\text{patients}} = 1,373$), shuffled with deterministic seed ($42$), and partitioned:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Partition:} 1,953 instances (85.0\%)
    \item \textbf{Calibration / Validation Partition:} 229 instances (10.0\%)
    \item \textbf{Held-Out Test Partition:} 459 instances (20.0\% of patients; 169 BCC, 146 ACK, 49 NEV, 47 SEK, 38 SCC, 10 MEL)
\end{itemize}
All transformations, median imputations, continuous feature scalers, and temperature parameters were fitted exclusively on the training partition and transferred without modification to validation and testing.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Neural Encoders}
\subsubsection{Visual Modality}
Photographs were standardized to $224 \times 224 \times 3$, normalized via ImageNet channel statistics, and augmented during training with affine rotations ($\pm 15^\circ$), horizontal/vertical reflections, and color jitter. Foreground lesion segmentation was initialized using GrabCut \cite{rother2004grabcut} to verify bounding compactness. The visual encoder combines a ResNet50 backbone \cite{he2016deep} with a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) Vision Transformer block \cite{dosovitskiy2021image} to capture both local dermoscopic textures and global structural asymmetry, yielding a 768-dimensional latent vector $z_{\text{img}}$.

\subsubsection{Clinical Metadata Modality}
Categorical covariates were one-hot encoded; binary symptoms were mapped to $\{0, 1\}$; continuous variables (age, diameter) were robustly scaled. Missing entries were imputed using training-set medians. The metadata encoder is a three-layer Residual MetaBlock MLP with batch normalization, dropout ($p=0.2$), and residual connections, producing a 256-dimensional representation $z_{\text{meta}}$.

\subsubsection{Clinical Narrative Text Modality}
To mirror teleconsultation intake notes, standardized narratives were generated from clinical questionnaires (e.g., \textit{``62-year-old female presenting with a 7mm pigmented lesion on the forearm; reports rapid enlargement and intermittent bleeding over 3 months''}). Texts were tokenized and embedded using domain-specific Bio\_ClinicalBERT \cite{alsentzer2019publicly}, with the 768-dimensional [CLS] representation projected to $z_{\text{txt}}$.

\subsection{Benchmark Ladder of Seven Classifiers}
We trained seven distinct classifiers covering every mathematical subset of modalities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model A (Image Only):} $f_{\text{img}}(z_{\text{img}}) \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model B (Metadata Only):} $f_{\text{meta}}(z_{\text{meta}}) \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model C (Text Only):} $f_{\text{txt}}(z_{\text{txt}}) \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model D (Image + Metadata):} Concatenation $[z_{\text{img}}, z_{\text{meta}}] \to \text{Linear} \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model E (Image + Text):} Concatenation $[z_{\text{img}}, z_{\text{txt}}] \to \text{Linear} \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model F (Metadata + Text):} Concatenation $[z_{\text{meta}}, z_{\text{txt}}] \to \text{Linear} \to \Delta^5$
    \item \textbf{Model G (Full Tri-Modal):} Concatenation $[z_{\text{img}}, z_{\text{meta}}, z_{\text{txt}}] \to \text{Linear} \to \Delta^5$
\end{itemize}
Models were optimized for 30 epochs using AdamW ($\text{weight decay} = 10^{-4}$), cosine annealing learning rate scheduler ($\eta_{\min} = 10^{-5}$), gradient clipping ($1.0$), and weighted cross-entropy.

\subsection{The 4-Tier Risk-Controlled Clinical Consensus Gate}
The consensus gate maps each incoming patient inquiry to a decisive triage action:
\begin{equation}
\text{Action} = \begin{cases}
\textbf{ESCALATE} & \text{if } S < 0.30 \text{ or } \{\text{BCC, SCC, MEL}\} \cap \mathcal{C}(x) \neq \emptyset \\
                  & \text{while } \arg\max \tilde{p} \text{ is non-malignant} \\
\textbf{ACQUIRE}  & \text{if } 0.30 \le S < 0.50 \text{ or } \tilde{p}_{\max} < 0.50 \\
\textbf{ACCEPT}   & \text{if } \tilde{p}_{\max} \ge 0.80 \text{ and agreement } a \ge 0.75 \\
\textbf{ARBITRATE}& \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Agent Layer and Look-Alike RAG System}
Six cooperating agents execute sequentially:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{IntakeAgent:} Validates incoming data, checks field bounds, and extracts structured entities.
    \item \textbf{SafetyAgent:} Screens text for red-flag terminology (e.g., ulceration, rapid bleeding) and verifies out-of-distribution distance via Mahalanobis covariance.
    \item \textbf{VisionTriageTeam:} Executes active neural backbones in parallel and applies evidence weighting.
    \item \textbf{LibrarianAgent (Visual Case-Based RAG):} Generates a fused 1536-dimensional embedding $[z_{\text{img}}, z_{\text{txt}}]$ and queries a FAISS vector index of all 1,953 training patients. The top-8 nearest confirmed look-alike patients are retrieved and visualized side-by-side with diagnostic concordance weighting.
    \item \textbf{ReferralAgent:} Translates risk categories into clinical urgency tiers (Immediate, 48-Hour, Routine).
    \item \textbf{ReportVerificationAgent:} Generates bilingual patient/physician summaries with citation grounding, audited by an internal rule-based consistency verifier.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Subgroup Fairness and Feature Attribution}
\subsubsection{Fitzpatrick Skin Phototypes}
Evaluating performance across skin tones revealed 91.2\% accuracy on Types I--II ($n=284$), 88.4\% on Types III--IV ($n=152$), and 82.6\% on Types V--VI ($n=23$). The drop on deeply pigmented lesions highlights dataset skew in PAD-UFES-20 and motivates future prospective collection on darker phototypes.

\subsubsection{SHAP Metadata Attribution}
TreeSHAP and KernelSHAP analysis on Model B identified patient age ($\text{mean } |\text{SHAP}| = 0.38$), bleeding history ($0.34$), lesion diameter ($0.29$), and lesion growth ($0.26$) as the most influential clinical covariates, mirroring established dermatological ABCD criteria.

\subsubsection{Learn-then-Test Risk Policy vs Baselines}
Comparing our LTT risk-controlled policy against standard softmax thresholding and Learning-to-Defer (L2D) \cite{mozannar2020consistent}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item DERMA-GUARD certified a \textbf{3.1\% missed-cancer rate} on confident predictions with 89.1\% case coverage.
    \item Learning-to-Defer yielded a 4.9\% miss rate ($p=0.011$ by paired permutation test).
    \item Softmax thresholding swung between 3.5\% and 9.3\% miss rates across different model backends, proving that heuristic thresholds fail under multimodal architecture shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Clinical Triage Utility}
The empirical findings demonstrate that multimodal synergy fundamentally stabilizes teleconsultation triage. While image-only models (Model A) struggle with morphological mimicry between early-stage BCC and actinic keratosis, incorporating structured patient history and clinical text (Model G) boosts overall accuracy to 89.76\% and macro sensitivity to 0.9456. Crucially, the 100\% melanoma recall observed across all architectures provides dermatologists with high confidence in primary screening.

\subsection{Dual-Modality Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)}
To overcome the black-box dilemma of deep neural networks in teledermatology, DERMA-GUARD embeds an operational \textit{Dual-Modality Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)} pipeline. Unlike pure text generative RAG frameworks, our architecture projects both the penultimate visual feature vectors (ResNet50, 2048-d mapped to 256-d) and the narrative semantic representations (Bio\_ClinicalBERT [CLS] token, 768-d mapped to 128-d) into a shared multimodal space. We populate a high-throughput FAISS index with all 1,953 histopathologically verified training lesions. During inference, when an incoming patient case exhibits borderline evidence sufficiency (.50 \le S < 0.70$), the FAISS engine computes dense L2 distances in under 10 milliseconds, retrieving the top-8 confirmed look-alike precedents with complete clinical context, biopsy confirmation status, and diagnostic outcome. The physician is presented with analogical proof rather than an isolated probability scalar, anchoring machine predictions to tangible clinical historical evidence and resolving ambiguous presentations.

\subsection{Agentic AI Consensus \& Autonomous Coordination}
DERMA-GUARD departs from monolithic end-to-end classifiers by adopting an \textit{Agentic AI} multi-specialist coordination structure. The system delegates discrete analytical responsibilities to autonomous, specialized sub-agents that collaborate over a structured evidence protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evidence Acquisition Agent:} Audits raw inputs for motion blur (Laplacian variance), illumination defects, and missing clinical fields before processing.
    \item \textbf{Specialist Diagnostic Agents:} Computer Vision, Tabular Clinical Risk, and NLP Symptom Narrative independently infer class probabilities and entropy uncertainties.
    \item \textbf{Consensus \& Fusion Agent:} Executes dynamic Bayesian evidence weighting in log-space ( \leftarrow (w_m^{0.6} \pi_m^{0.4})/Z$) based on observed quality ($), reliability ($), and cross-agent consistency ($).
    \item \textbf{Risk Control \& Deferral Agent:} Applies distribution-free Learn-then-Test (LTT) bounds and Mondrian conformal prediction sets, deciding autonomously whether to ACCEPT, ARBITRATE with RAG, ACQUIRE missing tests via Value-of-Information (VOI), or ESCALATE to a board-certified dermatologist.
\end{enumerate}
This agentic separation of concerns guarantees auditable fail-safes, preventing corrupted evidence from silently cascading into unsafe clinical recommendations.

\subsection{Role of Evidence Sufficiency ($)}
In telemedicine, corrupted inputs cause silent failures. By separating diagnostic classification from evidence scoring, DERMA-GUARD's Sufficiency gate ( < 0.30$) successfully intercepted degraded test queries, converting dangerous guesses into active acquisition recommendations (e.g., requesting a non-glare photo or clarifying lesion history).

\subsection{Limitations}
1) \textit{Melanoma Sample Size:} PAD-UFES-20 contains 10 melanomas in the test partition, reflecting Brazilian population prevalence. Multi-center validation on larger international cohorts is essential. 2) \textit{Synthetic Text:} Clinical symptom text was generated from standardized medical records rather than unconstrained transcribed physician dictation. 3) \textit{Fitzpatrick Skew:} Types V and VI comprise only 5\% of PAD-UFES-20.

\section{Conclusion}
DERMA-GUARD demonstrates that clinical multimodal AI must evaluate input quality and predictive uncertainty before rendering diagnostic verdicts. Through an agentic consensus framework integrating seven neural specialists, dynamic evidence weighting, Learn-then-Test statistical bounds, and dual-modality FAISS case retrieval (RAG), the system delivers 89.76\% accuracy, 100\% melanoma recall, and a certified $\le 3.1\%$ missed-cancer rate. Grounding uncertain predictions in verified historical cases via RAG provides transparent analogical proof for dermatologists, establishing a safe, interpretable paradigm for primary care teledermatology.
