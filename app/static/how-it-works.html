Method

How DERMA-GUARD reaches a decision

Most classifiers output one label with one confidence. DERMA-GUARD treats every input as evidence that can be good, weak or missing — and reasons about that before diagnosing.

1 · InputsPhoto · patient details · free text
2 · EncodersResNet50 + attention ViT · MetaBlock MLP · Bio_ClinicalBERT
3 · Evidence managerQ · R · U · C · S · M per source
4 · Adaptive fusionEvidence-weighted, 3 refinement iterations
5 · GateConformal set + 4-level consensus

The five evidence dimensions

Q

Quality

Image: sharpness, lighting, contrast, resolution, occlusion. Metadata: fraction of fields present. Text: informativeness score (PTIS).

R

Reliability

How stable the prediction is: low entropy, low variance under test-time augmentation, and agreement across models.

U

Utility

How informative a modality is for the currently suspected diagnosis — images matter most for melanoma, history for keratoses.

C

Consistency

Jensen-Shannon agreement between modality predictions. Contradicting sources lower trust in all of them.

S

Sufficiency

Overall: is there enough good evidence to decide at all? Drives the proceed / acquire / abstain gate.

M

Missingness

A hard mask. A source that is absent contributes exactly zero weight — no imputed guesses.

Drag the sliders to see how input quality changes the sufficiency gate and the fusion weights. The formulas are the ones the engine uses.

Four-level consensus

LevelActionWhenWhat happens
1ACCEPTGate passes, ≥75% of models agree, confidence ≥ 80%Result shown with supporting evidence
2ARBITRATEGate passes, confidence 50–80%Clinician should confirm
3ACQUIRESufficiency 0.30–0.50 or confidence < 50%Asks for a better photo or more details
4ESCALATESufficiency < 0.30, or a malignant class hides inside the conformal setRefers to a specialist; no automated label trusted
