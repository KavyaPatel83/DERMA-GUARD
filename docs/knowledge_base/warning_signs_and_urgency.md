# Warning signs and when to seek care

## The ABCDE rule
Look for Asymmetry, irregular Border, Colour variation, Diameter greater than about 6 millimetres, and Evolution over time. Any one of these features is a reason to have a spot examined. Evolution, meaning change, is the most important single sign.

## The ugly duckling sign
Most moles on one person look alike. A spot that looks clearly different from the others, darker, lighter, larger or differently shaped, deserves attention even if it does not meet every ABCDE criterion.

## Symptoms that should be assessed promptly
A sore that does not heal within four weeks. A lesion that bleeds without injury, crusts repeatedly, or becomes painful. A new growth that enlarges over weeks to months. A dark streak under or around a nail. A raised firm nodule that is growing quickly.

## Urgent or emergency situations
Seek urgent medical care the same day for a skin lesion with rapidly spreading redness, warmth, pus or fever, which suggests infection. Seek urgent care for heavy bleeding that does not stop with pressure. A rapidly growing, firm, dark or bleeding nodule should be seen within days rather than weeks. Call local emergency services for breathing difficulty, swelling of the face or throat, or fainting.

## Urgency levels used in this portal
Routine means an appointment within about four to six weeks. Soon means within one to two weeks. Urgent means within 48 hours to one week. Emergency means immediate care. DERMA-GUARD suggests an urgency level from the evidence it has, but a clinician always decides.

## What to bring to an appointment
Bring a list of when the spot appeared and how it has changed, any previous photographs, a list of medicines, personal and family history of skin cancer, and any previous biopsy reports. Photographs taken at the same distance and light over time are very helpful to the clinician.
