# Prevention and skin self-examination

## Sun protection
Ultraviolet radiation is the main modifiable cause of skin cancer. Seek shade between about 10 am and 4 pm when UV is strongest. Wear a broad-brimmed hat, sunglasses and tightly woven clothing. Apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher to exposed skin about 15 minutes before going outside, use about two tablespoons for the whole body, and reapply every two hours and after swimming or sweating. Avoid sunbeds. Children need particular care because sunburn in childhood raises melanoma risk.

## Monthly skin self-examination
Examine your whole skin once a month in a well-lit room with a full-length mirror and a hand mirror. Check the face, ears, scalp using a comb or hair dryer, neck, chest, arms, hands including between fingers and under nails, trunk, back of the legs, soles of the feet and between the toes, and the genital area. Note the number, size and pattern of moles. Take dated photographs of any spot you want to track so you can detect change objectively.

## Who is at higher risk
Fair skin that burns easily, a history of blistering sunburn, many or atypical moles, a personal or family history of melanoma or other skin cancers, older age, long outdoor work or exposure, weakened immune system such as after organ transplant, and previous radiation therapy. People with higher risk benefit from a professional full-body skin check, typically once a year or as advised.

## Skin of colour
Skin cancer is less common but often diagnosed later in people with darker skin, and it may occur in less sun-exposed sites such as palms, soles, under nails and mucous membranes. Check these areas as well.

## Photographing a lesion well
Use natural diffuse light, keep the lesion in focus and fill the frame, take one close-up and one wider image to show the location, avoid flash glare and filters, and place a ruler or coin beside it for scale if possible. Clean, sharp, well-lit photos improve the reliability of any image analysis.
